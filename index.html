How the map prediction works

The colored overlay guesses, for every spot in the room, how far the sound image lands from where the mix intended it. It adds up each speaker's direction weighted by its energy at that spot, and strongly discounts any speaker whose sound arrives more than about 1 ms after the first one (the precedence effect: your brain localizes on the first wavefront). Arrival spread shows the gap between first and last arrival. It is a teaching model, not a perceptual simulation: trust your ears over the colors.

Guesses and simplifications

  • Generic HRTF. The browser's built-in head model is not your head. Front and back can swap and height cues are weak, so screen speakers may feel close or inside your head. Turning your head with the slider helps.
  • Direct sound only, plus one generic reverb tail. Wall, glass and seat reflections are not modeled, and in a car they matter a lot.
  • Room sizes and speaker spots are typical guesses: an 18 × 28 m cinema of about 300 seats with stadium rows, and a mid-size car cabin.
  • Simple speaker directivity: tweeters lose level and treble off-axis with a cardioid-like rule; woofers barely, subwoofers not at all.
  • Calibration is delay and level only. Real systems also EQ every channel (the cinema X-curve, per-seat car tuning) and correct phase.
  • One crossover at 2.5 kHz for two-way car speakers, and all bass below 100 Hz is also sent to the subwoofer.
  • Dragging yourself while sound plays changes delays smoothly, so you hear a small pitch bend. That is real Doppler shift.
